**Focus:** Team Process Improvement

### Overview

Effective teams regularly reflect on how they're working together and make deliberate improvements. This checkpoint requires you to facilitate a team retrospective and document both the process and the outcomes.

### Requirements

## Facilitate a retrospective, then document the process and decisions:

- **Plan and Facilitate** Design and run a retrospective session for your team:
- Choose a format appropriate for your team's current needs (Start/Stop/Continue, 4Ls, Sailboat, etc.)
- Create space for all team members to contribute
- Focus on actionable improvements, not just venting
- Ensure psychological safety—retrospectives work best when people can be honest
**Logistics**: Reserve a space for your retro:
- 25Live for rooms across campus
- Pius Library rooms
- RH 115, workbay in ISE 210, or up to 4 spots in BSC 253 via #staff-and-leads
- **Document Your Process** **Facilitation Approach**:
- What retrospective format did you use and why?
- How did you structure the session?
- Include photos or diagrams if helpful (sticky notes, whiteboards, etc.)
**Data and Insights**:
- What data informed your team's discussion? (velocity, completed work, blockers, team feedback, incident reports, etc.)
- What themes emerged from the retrospective?
- What's working well? What needs improvement?
**Team-Level Improvements**:
- What element of your teamwork will you improve in the next two sprints?
- What specific, concrete steps will the team take to make this improvement?
- How will you know if the improvement is working?
**Individual Commitments**:
- What specific improvements has each team member committed to?
- How do individual commitments support team goals?
**Follow-Through Plan**:
- How will you track progress on these improvements?
- When will you check in on whether changes are happening?

### Client Involvement

Your client does not need to participate in the retrospective itself, but you may choose to share relevant outcomes with them.

### Deliverables

- Retrospective documentation addressing the elements above
- Evidence of the session (photos, notes, or artifacts from your retrospective)

### Evaluation Criteria

- **Complete and thoughtful documentation** (1 point): Appropriate facilitation format, specific and actionable improvements with clear ownership, evidence of genuine team engagement

### Notes

- The best retrospectives focus on the future (what we'll improve) not just the past (what went wrong)
- Make improvements specific and measurable—"communicate better" is vague, "respond to Slack messages within 4 hours during work days" is actionable
- Start small: one or two meaningful changes are better than a long list of aspirations
- Retrospectives should feel safe—what's said in the retro stays in the retro unless the team decides otherwise
