## Tech Lead Metrics Report

**Due:** October 29
**Sprint:** 4
**Focus:** Team Performance & Progress Tracking

### Overview

Effective technical leadership requires understanding how your team is performing and where improvements are needed. This checkpoint requires you to establish metrics tracking for your team and produce a report analyzing your progress. You'll produce these reports every two sprints throughout the semester.

### Requirements

## Determine what to track, collect data, and analyze your team's performance:

- **Determine Your Metrics** As a team, decide what metrics are meaningful for tracking your progress and performance. Consider metrics such as:
- **Velocity**: Work completed per sprint
- **WIP (Work in Progress)**: How much work is active at once
- **Code Coverage**: Percentage of code covered by tests
- **Defect Rate**: Bugs discovered per sprint or per feature
- **Goal Achievement**: Sprint goals met vs. planned
- **Burndown**: Progress toward sprint completion over time
- **Team Satisfaction**: How the team feels about their work and collaboration
- **Blocked Work**: Tasks waiting on dependencies or decisions
- **Customer Satisfaction**: Client feedback and sentiment
- **Time to Market**: How long features take from idea to deployment
**Minimum requirement**: Track at least one metric consistently. **Team decision**: Determine which metrics are most meaningful for your team's context, goals, and current challenges. You don't need to track everything—focus on what will actually inform your decisions. **Automation**: Automate data collection and analysis as much as possible. Manual tracking is acceptable initially, but identify opportunities to reduce manual overhead.
- **Produce a Metrics Report** Analyze your metrics and produce a report covering: **Metrics Selection**:
- What metrics did you choose to track and why?
- How do these metrics align with your team's goals and challenges?
**Baseline Establishment** (for first report):
- What are your current metric values?
- What data are you using to establish this baseline?
- Include visualizations where helpful (charts, graphs, burndown diagrams)
**Analysis** (for subsequent reports):
- What trends do you observe since the last report?
- What do these metrics tell you about team performance?
- What surprised you about the data?
- Is important context missing from the numbers?
**Action Items**:
- Based on these metrics, what should the team prioritize?
- What specific changes will you make in upcoming sprints?
- How will you know if those changes are working?
**Process Reflection**:
- How difficult was it to collect this data?
- What metrics were most/least valuable?
- What would you track differently next time?

### Deliverables

- **Metrics tracking system**: Documentation of what you're tracking and how
- **Metrics report**: Analysis addressing the elements above, with supporting data and visualizations

### Evaluation Criteria

- **Thoughtful metrics selection and meaningful analysis** (1 point): Team chose appropriate metrics, collected data consistently, and drew useful insights that inform team decisions

### Notes

- Start simple: it's better to track a few metrics well than many metrics poorly
- Metrics should inform decisions, not just create busywork
- Be honest about what the data shows—metrics exist to help you improve, not to make you look good
- Automation is a goal, but manual tracking is fine initially while you figure out what's worth measuring
- Consider what your retrospective revealed—do your metrics help you track the improvements you committed to?
- **For this first report**: Focus on establishing a clear baseline. Future reports will track trends and changes over time.
