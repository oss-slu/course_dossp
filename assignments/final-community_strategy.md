**Focus:** Program-Ready & Handoff-Ready Community Documentation

## Overview

Your Community Strategy is a required program deliverable that demonstrates your understanding of open source community building and documents your approach to creating a sustainable, welcoming community around your product. Unlike traditional software projects, open source success depends on effective community engagement, contribution management, and long-term sustainability planning.
This document should be polished, comprehensive, and ready to guide future maintainers in continuing the community-building work you've started.

## Requirements

Create a comprehensive Community Strategy document using the template in Appendix C. Your strategy should:

### Reflect Your Community Building Work

Build on:
- Learnings from your Community Building Checkpoint
- Ongoing community engagement throughout the semester
- Real community data (contributor patterns, response times, engagement levels)
- Stakeholder feedback from users, contributors, and clients
- Community management theory from readings, guest speakers, and course materials

### Include All Required Sections

See Appendix C for the complete template. Key sections include:
- Strategic Foundation (executive summary, vision, success metrics)
- Community Understanding (target members, needs/barriers, community health assessment)
- Contributor Experience (discovery, first impression, first contribution, ongoing engagement)
- Engagement Infrastructure (communication channels, content/outreach, recognition, onboarding)
- Governance and Sustainability (decision-making, code of conduct, handoff plan, resources)
- Strategic Execution (community activities, measurement, risks)

### Demonstrate Community Management Understanding

Strong Community Strategies:
- Grounded in your actual project context and maturity level
- Actionable and specific, not generic OSS advice
- Evidence-based, referencing actual community data and patterns
- Balanced consideration of user and contributor communities
- Sustainable plans for handoff and long-term maintenance
- Inclusive pathways for diverse contributors at different skill levels

### Align with Product Strategy

Your Community Strategy should support your Product Strategy's goals, make sense given your product's technical approach and target users, and balance community growth with quality and sustainability.

### Meet Professional Standards

Your document should be well-organized, clearly written, appropriately detailed, properly formatted, and error-free.

## Deliverables

- Community Strategy (dated PDF)
- Brief cover note highlighting key changes from your draft and how your community evolved during the semester

## Evaluation Criteria

## Community Strategy Quality

- Clear, achievable vision for community growth
- Specific tactics tailored to your project's context and maturity
- Realistic assessment of current community health with evidence
- Thoughtful contributor journey design that reduces friction

## Practical Grounding

- Strategy reflects actual experience building your community
- Incorporates lessons from Community Building Checkpoint
- Based on real data about contributors, response times, engagement
- Honest about current state and challenges

## Open Source Best Practices

- Strong contribution guidelines and documentation
- Appropriate governance model for project maturity
- Clear code of conduct and moderation approach
- Multiple pathways for contribution at different skill levels

## Sustainability Planning

- Thoughtful handoff plan for future maintainers
- Succession planning and knowledge transfer
- Documentation that enables continuation
- Realistic resource requirements

## Professional Quality

- Program-ready polish and presentation
- Clear, well-organized documentation
- Error-free, well-edited final product

## Notes

- **This is a final deliverable:** Should be polished and ready for program assessment
- **Incorporate feedback:** Address feedback from your Strategy Draft
- **Update with experience:** Reflect how your community evolved since the draft
- **Be honest about maturity:** New projects have different community needs than mature ones
- **Connect to Product Strategy:** Your community strategy should advance your product goals
- **Think handoff:** Future maintainers need to understand not just what to do, but why
- **Make it sustainable:** Plans that depend on heroic effort from future teams will fail
