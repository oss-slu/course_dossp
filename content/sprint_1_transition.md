## 1. The Machine

We have been doing a lot to get projects ready to start working on, and a lot to start getting our project teams up and running. I, as both Program Director and course Instructor, have been very hands on with everyone, trying to build The Machine. The Machine is all the process and planning and infrastructure that enables us to operate smoothly during our sprints. There is enough work to be done on the projects themselves that The Machine needs to be in good working order or it becomes a distraction from the work. Now that Sprint 1 starts, the machine should be running smoothly.

## 2. My Focus Shifts

Now that The Machine is in place I will be stepping back from pushing, and will move into more of an observational mode. I also have a project team #project_smartrobot and an internal developer services team #project_infra_ops that I will be working directly with. I'll also shift some of my time to meeting directly with each Developer. There are over 60 of them, and my goal is to meet with them 1:1 at least once before mid-terms and at least one more time before the end of the semester. My conversations with them will be one of the ways I gather information for the performance evaluations of each Tech Lead. I also have Program Director responsibilities that I need to shift some of my focus to (various special events, advancement of the Rerum OSE, and building relationships with new clients and partners).

## 3. Your New Responsibilities

Now that the machine is in place, that means a few things for you as a Tech Lead:
- You should have more time to work with your team on helping them succeed on the work itself.
- You should have more time to work on the parts of the project that are either especially interesting to you, or that the team may not be able to tackle themselves.
- You should have more time to work on all the other things that are on your plate. I want to make sure you are maintaining a sustainable pace, and not getting burned out.
Let me be entirely clear about what is not your responsibility with regard to this course. You are not in Capstone, so you are not responsible for the work of a CS Capstone student. You are not a TA for Capstone, and you do not need to prepare problems with known solutions. For the work you are doing here you are not a TA or RA for your client (if you have one), and you do not need to assist them with their courses or directly engage in their research.
Your core responsibility is to the product you are building. You are stewarding the development of this product through your Technical Leadership. You are driving towards the long-term success of the product by actively engaging in the success of this project.
These are now YOUR projects. You need to own them, drive the conversation, and provide technical leadership to your team. There are a few exceptions for anyone working in completely new models, or anyone who joined relatively late. I'll continue providing you more hands on support during Sprint 1. I'll evaluate readiness at the end of the sprint, but my goal for everyone is for you to take the reigns of control.
Ownership means you are responsible for the setting and maintaining the tone, standards, and norms. This includes:
- Making technical architecture decisions for your project
- Facilitating team meetings and sprint planning
- Identifying when your team needs help and finding appropriate resources
- Setting coding standards and review processes for your project
- Communicating project status and blockers to stakeholders
- Mentoring developers on technical skills specific to your project domain
- Etcetera - every project will have unique context-specific responsibilities that emerge as you progress
Leadership means you are literally ahead of your team. You're thinking ahead of them, working ahead of them, and ready to step to the front when no one else is able to.

## 4. Support Systems

**Direct Mentoring Support** There are a couple of mechanisms in place for direct mentorship support for each of you. I am available and around still, but I just won't be pushing Tech Leads quite so directly. The Industry Fellows are around, and were originally conceived of as another mentor support for Tech Leads in particular. Once Santosh is officially hired, he'll set up a regular rotation to meet with every Tech Lead—for at least 30 minutes, and up to an hour—at some point during each Sprint. Because he was once a Tech Lead, he knows many of the challenges you will inevitably face. In talking with him, I have found he has lots of good ideas for practical solutions. Talk through the specific challenges you are facing with your team. One of the things Santosh has already demonstrated mastery of is the social dynamics of software development. He has his own technical expertises within software engineering, and may not be able to answer your technical problems depending on the nature of your project. Even if he doesn't have the answer, he can probably help you find and connect with a resource or a person who can help. Santosh is an ally for you. Your conversations with him will have no impact on your performance evaluation, so feel free to share information freely with him. Santosh will reach out to you at some point soon to start scheduling those meetings. Because he needs to schedule multiple meetings, please share your full availability with him rather than a single time you're available. 
**Communication Channels:** While "my door is always open" is cliché, the cliché is appropriate for an open source program. If you have questions, please ask in the open. If you need help, please, ask in the open. You can ask in your project channel, or you can ask in #help-industry_fellows, or you can ask in #staff-and-leads. If, for some reason, it's not the sort of thing you want to discuss in the open, you can DM me, too. I would simply prefer you start with asking in the open, and then ask privately only if necessary. I've said it before, and I'll say it again, "DMs can quickly become toxic for everyone involved." Use them with extreme caution!
**Evaluation and Milestones:** Key milestones for projects and project teams are all posted in Canvas. That's our "employee management" platform. You all have a performance evaluation schedule. The Developers have a separate performance evaluation schedule. I'll share with you a little more detail about Developer evaluations so you can have interactions with them informed by what they are thinking about. There are sprint-based progress reports that each Developer needs to stay on top of. One of those is checkpoint documents, many of which are the same documents for both Tech Leads and Developers, especially initially. I will probably make quick announcements to everyone about checkpoint documents at the start and end of class on Monday. The other parts are demo videos and personal reflections. Mohammad is the grader for CS Capstone. He's going to help me with validation of the sprint-based progress reports about the work completed during sprints based on clearly defined rubrics. Basically, to oversimplify the details, "do the demo videos match the PRs and Issues". I'll be doing qualitative assessments of all the other elements. Keep in mind Mohammad is focused on evaluation of the Developer work, and he is not a TA. If anyone has any questions about grading, they should talk with me. If it's about grading generally, please ask in the open. If it's about a specific grade, a DM is fine. I'll schedule 1:1s with Tech Leads to review each of your evaluations and I'm open to meeting with Developers about them if necessary. 
**DOSSP Course Elements:** In terms of the DOSSP course, we have not touched on any of the readings identified so far. The readings are entirely for your own growth and development as a professional. I'm not asking for anything specific from them, but we will have conversations during class about topics related to them. We'll also have various guests. Please, get engaged with the conversations. I don't want to dominate those conversations, and I'll probably ask lots of questions and try to avoid giving "the answer". These topics all have plenty of "it depends" qualifiers to what makes sense. I'm hoping you all can talk through those, exploring, asking questions, sharing ideas, offering opinions. I try to have strong opinions loosely held about professional topics. I have found it tends to be useful, but I also know I'm not always right (another strong opinion loosely held). If you are not the sort of person who feels comfortable speaking up, even in a room of about 20 people, then I encourage you to write your thoughts somewhere. It's great if you post them publicly to a personal blog, vlog, podcast, social media, or whatever. You can, at an absolute minimum, write something and share it directly with me if you think you may not be participating in class discussions enough.

## 5. Expectations Going Forward

Just because I'm not pushing for things doesn't mean the project channels should go silent. In fact, the more you engage the more likely I am to speak up with small details. If you are not driving the conversation, my job titles mean me speaking up too much relative to you speaking up robs you of your leadership. Also, part of our responsibility is to work in the open to support nascent open source communities that are forming around our products. Keep the conversation going.
Just because I'm not pushing for things doesn't mean I'm not expecting them. Now that we are past the onboarding period, I expect you to identify expectations and deliver without excessive prompting. That's table stakes for professionals. I'm just taking note in my professional evaluations. Professionalism also means you speak up if you're unsure, again, ask in the open. You can support each other in this space too. That's, literally, why professional associations like ACM, US-RSE and similar groups exist.
To be entirely clear, my expectations for you include:
- Regular work performance
- Issues are well defined enough that the team can finish refining them and start working by the end of the Monday session at the start of a sprint
- Timely responses to Pull Requests with thoughtful and constructive code reviews
- Timely merging of Pull Requests that meet all coding standards and contribute to product value
- Posting regular project updates in your channel (even brief ones)
- Ensuring that all key stakeholders have relevant information, including clients, developers, and any additional partners
- Ongoing communication
- Asking questions when you encounter blockers rather than struggling silently
- Sharing interesting technical discoveries or solutions with your team and other Tech Leads
- Facilitating sprint retrospectives and driving towards continuous improvement for the whole team
- Documenting decisions and rationale for future reference
- Responding to requests for help from other Tech Leads when you have relevant experience
- Communicating as soon as possible when you are unable to meet normal expectations
- Strategic oversight and ownership
- Identifying a product vision that aligns with OSS programmatic requirements, satisfies the immediate needs of any key stakeholders (like a client), and is inspiring to you, your team, and the target users
- Developing a comprehensive product strategy that drives towards a product vision
- Developing and maintaining a product architecture, and stewarding the technical decisions to align with the overall strategy
- Developing and maintaining a community strategy that drives adoption of the product by users and contributions from develpers
- Aligning with the culture we strive to maintain within Open Source with SLU
- Supporting open science by doing open science, especially open source software
- Using a learning mindset to bring experience and imagination to meet challenges rather than thinking there is a singular "answer"
- Using a growth mindset to bring enthusiasm and determination to meet challenges rather than thinking "that's just the ways it is"
- Etcetera - professional engagement varies by individual strengths and team dynamics, but the expectation is active participation in the community
**My biggest expectation** is in your ultimate success. I am very confident in your abilities, and I want you to achieve great things. Go forth and be excellent!
