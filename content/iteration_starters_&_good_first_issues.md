## Objectives:

What GitHub refers to as an "Issue" is sometimes referred to as a "ticket" or a "card", and we will use the terms interchangeably. Your objective here is to ensure you have work in your GitHub Issues backlog that is well defined and ready for contributions from both outside contributors as well as your core development team. You will do this by ensuring you have a sufficient number of " good first issue " and " iteration starter " cards, and that they are defined well enough so that someone can work them without requiring any additional information (from you or the client).
Submit a link to your project's GitHub Issues.

### [ ] Good First Issue 

The label good first issue is often used by open source software projects to indicate that a particular issue is simple and accessible to a developer who is relatively novice, and who is new to the project. It is usually a very clearly defined task with a very limited scope of work. What exactly that looks like can vary across projects. These issues are supplements to your core objectives for the product, and do not need to fit within any particular iteration of work.
Acceptance Criteria
- At least three cards in your project have a good first issue label
- Each good first issue card is a meaningful issue that contributes to the overall development of your project.
- Each good first issue card is a small problem that can be completed by an outside newcomer in about 1-2 hours.
- Each good first issue card is clearly defined so that someone who is new to the project can complete it without additional information. Communication with this developer might be a distraction, and should be minimized.

### [ ] Iteration Starter 

We will use the label iteration starter to indicate that a particular issue is simple and accessible to a developer who is a colleague in your core development team. They might be new to the project, or needs some practice getting started at the start of an iteration of sprints. It is usually a well defined task with a moderate scope of work. Ideally the work enables someone who is new to the project to become familiar with the software more fully. It can also be work that prepares your team for the work they will do over the full 6 sprint iteration. What exactly that looks like can vary across projects. These issues should be included in your product planning board so that your team completes them at the start of an iteration of sprints.
Acceptance Criteria
- At least three cards in your project have an iteration starter label
- Each iteration starter card is a meaningful issue that contributes to the overall development of your project during this iteration.
- Each iteration starter card is a small problem that can be completed by one of your colleagues on your team in one sprint of work.
- Each iteration starter card is typically defined so that someone who is new to the project can complete it without additional information, but it can require some additional information from you or the client that is not public information. Communication and collaboration with this developer are expected and encouraged, as it contributes to team building.
