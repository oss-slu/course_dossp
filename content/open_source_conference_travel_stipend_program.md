## **Program Overview**

The Open Source Conference Travel Stipend program provides financial support for students to attend major open source and open science conferences. This competitive program recognizes students who demonstrate exceptional engagement with open source principles, contribute to the broader research community, or show professional excellence in domains related to (but distinct from) their capstone projects.

### **Program Goals** ###

* Support student professional development in open source and open science communities
* Recognize excellence in contributions beyond coursework requirements
* Build connections between academic work and broader professional communities
* Encourage knowledge sharing and community engagement
* Promote diverse pathways to open source leadership

### **Key Program Features** ###

* **Voluntary participation** - no penalty for non-participation
* **No impact on course grades** - completely separate from coursework
* **Merit-based selection** - evaluated by faculty committee
* **Limited availability** - competitive selection process
* **Community focus** - recipients share knowledge with OSS community

## **Eligibility Requirements**

### **Core Requirements** ###

All applicants must meet the following criteria:

1. **Enrollment Status**

* Currently enrolled in CS Capstone or related Open Source with SLU program
* In good academic standing

3. **Work Scope Requirement**

* Demonstrated engagement with open source or open science principles
* Work must be **adjacent to but outside the direct scope** of capstone project
* Clear differentiation from required coursework

5. **Domain Adjacency Rule**

* Contribution can be in a related domain but **not directly part of capstone work**
* Example: A capstone team member working on a bioinformatics project contributes to general bioinformatics open source tools or documentation
* Example: A student working on a web application capstone contributes to a separate open source library in the same technology stack

7. **Community Engagement**

* Willingness to document and share conference experience
* Commitment to presenting learnings to OSS community
* Agreement to social media documentation requirements

### **Disqualifying Factors** ###

* Work that is part of required capstone project deliverables
* Contributions made solely for course credit
* Previous non-compliance with stipend program requirements (if applicable)
* Inability to attend full conference or meet reporting obligations

## **Application Categories**

Applicants should select the category that best describes their primary contribution. Work may span multiple categories, but applications should focus on the most significant achievement.

### **1. Open Source Contributions** ###

Recognition for meaningful contributions to open source projects and communities.

**Qualifying Activities:**

* Contributing code, documentation, or community support to established open source projects
* Creating new open source tools or libraries in your domain area
* Participating in open source governance, standards development, or community leadership
* Organizing or facilitating open source community events
* Significant bug fixes, feature implementations, or architectural improvements
* Translating documentation or making projects more accessible

**Evidence to Provide:**

* GitHub/GitLab contribution history and pull request links
* Project maintainer testimonials or community recognition
* Metrics showing adoption or impact (downloads, stars, forks, citations)
* Documentation of community engagement (forum posts, issue triage, code reviews)

### **2. Open Science Advancement** ###

Recognition for work that advances open science principles and practices.

**Qualifying Activities:**

* Publishing research, white papers, or technical documentation that advances open science
* Contributing to open data initiatives, reproducible research practices, or FAIR data principles
* Developing educational resources or training materials for open science communities
* Participating in or organizing open science workshops, conferences, or meetups
* Creating or contributing to open access publications
* Building tools for research reproducibility or data sharing

**Evidence to Provide:**

* Published papers, preprints, or technical reports (with links)
* Dataset releases with proper documentation and licensing
* Workshop materials, tutorials, or educational content
* Citations or adoption metrics
* Community feedback or testimonials

### **3. Rerum OSE Strategic Advancement** ###

Recognition for work that expands the Rerum ecosystem and advances universal annotation goals.

**Qualifying Activities:**

* Contributing to Rerum ecosystem expansion through integration work
* Developing tools, plugins, or extensions that expand Rerum's reach
* Creating educational content, documentation, or case studies for Rerum adoption
* Building partnerships or collaborations that advance Rerum's universal annotation goals
* Implementing Rerum in new contexts or domains
* Contributing to Rerum standards or specification development

**Evidence to Provide:**

* Code repositories showing Rerum integration or extension
* Documentation or tutorials created
* Partnership agreements or collaboration outcomes
* Adoption metrics or user testimonials
* Presentations or publications about Rerum applications

### **4. Professional Excellence in Domain** ###

Recognition for professional development activities that demonstrate leadership and expertise.

**Qualifying Activities:**

* Speaking at conferences, meetups, or professional events in your field
* Publishing technical blog posts, tutorials, or educational content
* Participating in professional organizations or standards bodies
* Mentoring other students or early-career professionals in your domain
* Contributing to academic research or industry best practices
* Organizing community events or study groups

**Evidence to Provide:**

* Conference talk recordings, slides, or acceptance letters
* Published articles or blog posts (with analytics if available)
* Mentorship testimonials or program documentation
* Professional organization involvement documentation
* Metrics showing community impact (views, engagement, citations)

* What you created, contributed, or accomplished

1. **Domain Connection (250 words)** Explain how your work relates to but differs from your capstone project. Address:

* How your contribution connects to your domain of study
* Clear differentiation from capstone project requirements
* Why this work is separate from coursework obligations
* How it complements but doesn't duplicate academic work

1. **Impact Evidence (250 words)** Document your contribution's reach or influence. Provide:

* Quantitative metrics (downloads, citations, users, views, etc.)
* Qualitative feedback (testimonials, reviews, community response)
* Adoption indicators (who is using your work, how, and why)

* Specific sessions, workshops, or tracks relevant to your contribution
* Networking opportunities aligned with your work
* How conference attendance will enhance your contribution
* Potential collaborations or partnerships you hope to develop

1. **Professional Development Plan (150 words)** Describe how you'll share conference learnings with the OSS community. Address:

* Specific format for sharing (lunch-and-learn, blog post, workshop, etc.)

* Key takeaways you expect to share
* How you'll apply conference learnings to future work

## **Evaluation Criteria**

Applications are evaluated holistically by a faculty committee using the following criteria:

### **Quality and Significance**  ###

* **Technical excellence:** Sophistication and quality of the contribution
* **Innovation:** Originality or novel approach to problems
* **Completeness:** Thoroughness of execution and documentation
* **Professional standards:** Adherence to community best practices

### **Clear Differentiation from Capstone**  ###

* **Scope separation:** Work is clearly outside capstone requirements
* **Domain adjacency:** Maintains relevant connection while being distinct
* **Independent motivation:** Driven by personal or community goals, not coursework
* **Additional effort:** Represents work beyond course obligations

### **Evidence of Impact** ###

* **Community reach:** Number and diversity of users, readers, or participants
* **Measurable outcomes:** Quantitative metrics demonstrating adoption or influence
* **Qualitative feedback:** Testimonials, reviews, or community recognition
* **Sustainability:** Evidence of ongoing use or continued development

### **Alignment with Open Source/Science Principles** ###

* **Open licensing:** Appropriate use of open source or creative commons licenses
* **Community engagement:** Active participation in open communities
* **Knowledge sharing:** Commitment to documentation and education
* **Ethical practices:** Adherence to community codes of conduct

### **Professional Growth Potential and Conference Fit** ###

* **Conference alignment:** Clear connection between work and conference focus
* **Learning objectives:** Well-articulated goals for conference attendance
* **Network development:** Realistic plans for professional connections
* **Career advancement:** Potential impact on future opportunities

### **Commitment to Knowledge Sharing** ###

* **Sharing plan quality:** Concrete, realistic plan for sharing learnings
* **Community benefit:** Clear value for broader OSS community
* **Social media engagement:** Willingness and ability to document experience
* **Follow-through likelihood:** Track record of completing commitments

### **Additional Considerations** ###

* **Diversity of contributions:** Committee seeks variety across categories
* **First-time attendees:** Preference given when candidates are equally qualified
* **Timing considerations:** Conference deadlines and registration requirements
* **Budget constraints:** Total number of awards limited by available funding

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Eligibility Questions** ###

**Q: Can I apply if I'm working on a capstone project?** A: Yes, but your stipend application must be for work that is adjacent to but outside the scope of your capstone project. The work should complement your capstone but be clearly separate from course requirements.

**Q: What does "domain adjacent" mean exactly?** A: Domain adjacent means your contribution is in a related area but not directly part of your capstone deliverables. For example, if your capstone involves building a web application, contributing to an open source JavaScript library used in similar applications would be domain adjacent.

**Q: Can I apply for work I did before enrolling in the course?** A: Yes, as long as the work meets all other eligibility criteria and you can demonstrate its significance and impact. The work should still be relevant to your current academic focus.

**Q: I'm not sure if my work qualifies. Should I apply anyway?** A: Yes! Apply and explain your work clearly. The review committee will determine eligibility. It's better to apply and let the committee decide than to self-select out.

### **Application Questions** ###

**Q: Can I submit multiple applications for different conferences?** A: Yes, you may submit multiple applications if you have distinct contributions that align with different conferences. However, each application must stand on its own merits.

**Q: Can I resubmit if I'm not selected?** A: Yes, you may revise and resubmit for future conference cycles. Use feedback (if provided) to strengthen your application.

**Q: What if I exceed the word count limits?** A: Minor variations (10-15%) are acceptable, but significantly exceeding limits may result in your application not being fully reviewed. Focus on concise, impactful writing.

**Q: Can someone review my application before I submit?** A: Yes! Seek feedback from peers, mentors, or instructors. However, ensure the final submission represents your own work and voice.

**Q: Do I need to have already registered for the conference?** A: No, do not register until after you've been notified of award selection. If selected, you'll receive instructions on registration.

### **Selection Questions** ###

**Q: How many stipends will be awarded?** A: The number varies based on application quality and available funding. It is competitive, and not all qualified applicants may receive awards.

**Q: What if multiple people apply for the same conference?** A: Multiple students may receive stipends for the same conference. Selection is based on individual merit, not conference choice.

**Q: Is preference given to seniors or certain majors?** A: No, all eligible students are considered equally. Selection is based on contribution quality and impact, not year or major.

**Q: How much does the "first-time conference attendee" preference matter?** A: It's a tie-breaker when candidates are equally qualified. If you've attended conferences before, you can still receive an award based on merit.

**Q: Will I receive feedback if not selected?** A: Due to the volume of applications, detailed feedback may not be provided for all applications. However, you may request brief feedback by emailing oss@slu.edu.

### **Award and Travel Questions** ###

**Q: What expenses are covered by the stipend?** A: Conference registration, travel (airfare, mileage, transit), lodging, and meals during the conference. All expenses require documentation for reimbursement.

**Q: What if my expenses exceed $500?** A: The stipend covers up to $500. You are responsible for any costs beyond that amount. Budget carefully when planning travel.

**Q: When do I receive the money?** A: Reimbursement is processed after you submit complete expense documentation following the conference. Processing typically takes 2-4 weeks.

**Q: What if I need to cancel due to an emergency?** A: Contact the program administrator immediately. Legitimate emergencies (medical, family crisis) may qualify for exceptions. Documentation will be required.

**Q: Can I use the stipend for a conference outside the listed options?** A: Yes, domain-specific conferences are acceptable if they meet program requirements and advance professional excellence in your domain. Explain the conference choice clearly in your application.

**Q: Do I need to book my own travel?** A: Yes, recipients are responsible for all travel arrangements. Book early for best rates. Save all receipts for reimbursement.

### **Social Media Questions** ###

**Q: What if I don't use social media?** A: Social media documentation is a requirement of the program. You may create professional accounts specifically for this purpose. LinkedIn is the minimum recommended platform.

**Q: Can I use a pseudonym or remain semi-anonymous?** A: Yes, you may use a professional pseudonym if preferred. Discuss your preferences with the program administrator.
