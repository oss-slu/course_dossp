This page provides details for the **poster session presentation** format, used as the Public Showcase in spring semesters at the SSE Undergraduate Showcase. See the [Team Presentation](../assignments/final-team_presentation.md) assignment for the full two-event arc and core expectations.

## Format

- Poster session, not a lecture — visitors arrive, stop, leave at will
- You give many short, conversational pitches, not one structured talk
- Think of it as a trade fair booth (for some projects) or a research exhibit (for others)

**Note for Graduate Students:** While this is administratively an undergraduate showcase, graduate students are welcome and encouraged to participate.

## Poster Approach by Project Track

### Entrepreneurship Track

- Trade-show approach encouraged
- Think trade fair booth, not academic paper
- Augment around the poster — utilize the full table space for compelling trade-show style displays
- Your instructor will confirm which projects are on this track

### Client-Driven Product Track

- Approach depends on the nature of your client and product
- Could go trade-show or research-style
- Discuss with your Tech Lead and instructor which framing fits

### Research Track

- Standard research poster approach
- Lean into academic norms: methodology, findings, implications
- Your instructor will confirm whether your project falls on this track

## SSE Showcase Logistics

The team member who completed the SSE Showcase Presenter Registration will receive a details email from **sseug@slu.edu** with the current year's date, time, location, setup window, and poster submission deadline. Share that email with your full team.

What to expect from the organizer email:

- **Date, time, and location** of the showcase
- **Setup window** — when teams must arrive and be ready
- **Poster PDF submission deadline** — typically a week or more before the event
- **What's provided** — easel, backing board, clips (historically; confirm from the email)
- **Schedule** — viewing/judging period, awards ceremony

What your team is responsible for regardless:

- **Printing your poster** (IMC in the Library is the typical campus option)
- **Bringing your poster** to the venue
- **Power strip / extension cord** if you requested electricity
- **Professional dress**
- **Career Services** invites industry professionals — job-seeking presenters can get a name tag sticker signaling openness to career conversations

## Poster Specifications

- **Size:** 42" x 32" (required by college)
- Must include university logo per the official SSE template
- Templates available as a starting point — they are a floor, not a ceiling:
  - **PowerPoint template** — provided by the SSE Showcase organizers (see your instructor)
  - **LaTeX template** — available for teams that prefer it, especially useful for research-track posters (see your instructor)
- Visual communication is a learning objective
- **Encouraged:** Augment around the poster and utilize the full table space, especially for trade-show style displays

## Poster Content Sections

### Required Sections

**The Problem & Value Proposition**
What problem does your product solve? For whom? Why does it matter that it gets solved? In one sentence: what does your product do that nothing else does as well?

**Product Overview**
What is your product? Include a screenshot or diagram of the product in use. This section should be immediately legible to someone who knows nothing about your project.

**Key Features**
The 3–5 things your product does that deliver the most value. These should connect directly to the problem statement above.

**System Architecture**
A diagram showing how the major components of your system fit together. Keep it accessible — this is for a mixed audience, not a technical review.

**What We Built**
What did your team accomplish this iteration? Frame this as contribution, not status. What value exists now that didn't before?

**Future Work**
What remains to be done? What would another iteration enable?

### Conditional Sections

**Professional Impact** *(include if your team shares common career themes)*
What skills does this project demonstrate? What professional capabilities did you develop as a team? Individual career articulation — what *you* specifically contributed and what it demonstrates — is something you should be ready to speak to, even if it's not on the poster.

**Research Impact** *(include if your project is research software)*
What does your software enable that wasn't possible before? Who benefits in a research context? What questions can now be asked or answered?

## Pitch Expectations

- 2–3 minute conversational pitch, repeated many times
- Adapt for technical and non-technical audiences
- All team members able to give the full pitch
- Sustain energy across repetitions
- Career articulation: be ready to explain your specific contributions

## Demo Expectations

- Live demo on demand — working software on a device
- Have a fallback plan (recorded video, screenshots)
- Adapt demo depth for the audience

## Audience

- Faculty and academic stakeholders
- Industry guests and potential employers
- Fellow students from across the School of Science and Engineering
- Your client (if attending)

**Your goal:** A faculty judge and a recruiter should both walk away knowing what you built, why it matters, and what you're capable of.

## Deliverables

- Printed poster (brought to venue)
- A device ready for live demo
- Brief reflection (1 paragraph per team member)

## Bonus: Slide Deck, Script, and Trial Run

Teams that want to go further can produce a full presentation package alongside their poster. This is optional and not required for full credit, but it is strongly encouraged for teams with a client presentation, a research audience, or career portfolios in mind.

### Presentation Script with Speaker Notes

A pitch guide covering:

- **Speaker assignments:** Who covers which sections
- **Talking points:** Key messages for each section (not word-for-word, but enough to rehearse from)
- **Transitions:** How you'll hand off between speakers
- **Backup plans:** How you'll handle questions or technical difficulties

### Slide Deck

A visual presentation (PowerPoint, Google Slides, or similar) that includes:

- Clear, professional slides supporting your narrative
- Screenshots showing your product in action
- Demo clips or screen recordings (if applicable)
- Minimal text per slide — visuals support speaking, not replace it
- Consistent formatting and professional design

### Trial Run and Feedback Documentation

Conduct at least one complete trial run and document:

- **Recording:** Video of your trial run (doesn't need to be polished, but should be complete)
- **Self-assessment:** What worked well? What needs improvement?
- **Peer feedback:** Have someone outside your team watch and respond
- **Improvement plan:** Specific changes you'll make before the showcase
