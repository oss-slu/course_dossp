**Focus:** Initial Strategy Development & Iteration

## Overview

Your Product Strategy and Community Strategy documents are essential deliverables that guide current development decisions, communicate vision to stakeholders, and provide foundation for future teams. This checkpoint requires you to draft both strategies and receive feedback before creating final, polished versions.
Well-crafted strategy documents synthesize your continuous discovery work, stakeholder inputs, experimental learnings, and technical insights into coherent direction. They should be living documents that evolve as you learn more about user needs, community dynamics, and technical possibilities.

## Requirements

Submit initial drafts of both your Product Strategy and Community Strategy documents:

### 1. Product Strategy Draft

Create an initial Product Strategy that includes relevant information about your strategy for building out the capabilities of your product. Adapt the details to suit your particular product. You should probably include:

## Core sections:

- Executive Summary
- Product Vision (vision statement, mission, success criteria)
- User Research and Needs (target users, pain points, personas if applicable)
- Market and Competitive Analysis
- Product Positioning (value proposition, key features)
- Product Roadmap (current release, next release, future direction)
- Technical Approach (architecture overview, constraints, risks)

## Your draft should:

- Be informed by the product and community management work performed during the iteration
- Draw from your continuous discovery work (user interviews, client conversations, community feedback)
- Build on insights from your Usability Research and Market Evaluation checkpoints
- Explain the "why" behind product decisions, not just the "what"
- Be grounded in real user needs and evidence, not assumptions
**Note: **You can start by using the template in Appendix C of the Tech Lead handbook (currently in draft form, available via request). 

### 2. Community Strategy Draft

Create an initial Community Strategy that tells how your product will build a community of users and a community of contributors. Adapt the details to suit your particular product. Your strategy hould probably include:

## Core sections:

- Executive Summary
- Community Vision (vision statement, goals, success metrics)
- Target Community Members (contributor personas, user personas)
- Community Health Assessment (current state, strengths, opportunities)
- Contributor Journey (discovery, first impression, first contribution, ongoing engagement)
- Community Engagement Tactics (communication channels, content/outreach, recognition)
- Governance and Decision-Making
- Sustainability and Handoff

## Your draft should:

- Build on learnings from your Community Building Checkpoint
- Be informed by the product and community management work performed during the iteration
- Reflect your actual project context and maturity level
- Include concrete, actionable tactics (not just aspirational goals)
**Note: **You can start by using the template in Appendix C of the Tech Lead handbook (currently in draft form, available via request). 

### 3. Self-Assessment & Questions

Along with your drafts, submit a brief self-assessment (1 page max):
- **Confidence levels:** Which sections feel solid? Which need more work?
- **Evidence gaps:** Where are you making assumptions vs. working from data?
- **Open questions:** What do you need feedback on? What are you uncertain about?
- **Specific feedback requests:** What areas do you most want reviewers to focus on?

## Deliverables

- Product Strategy Draft (dated PDF or link to markdown in your repository)
- Community Strategy Draft (dated PDF or link to markdown in your repository)
- Self-Assessment (1 page)

## Evaluation Criteria

## Substance over Polish

- Drafts show serious thinking about your product and community
- Evidence of synthesis from discovery work, client input, community feedback, and technical constraints
- Grounded in your actual project context, not generic templates
- Clear integration of learnings from previous checkpoint documents

## Strategic Clarity

- Clear problem statements and value propositions
- Logical connection between user needs and product decisions
- Community strategy aligns with product goals and project maturity

## Completeness

- All required sections present with meaningful content
- Sufficient detail to guide decisions while allowing flexibility
- Honest acknowledgment of gaps and uncertainties

## Self-Awareness

- Thoughtful self-assessment of draft quality
- Clear identification of areas needing feedback
- Specific questions that will improve final versions

## Notes

- **This is a draft:** Expect to revise based on feedback. Polish comes later.
- **Use the templates:** Adapt them to your project, but don't skip sections without good reason. If you do leave something out, try to articulate a reason in your self assessment.
- **Build on previous work:** Reference your Community Building Checkpoint, Usability Research, and Market Evaluation learnings.
- **Get stakeholder input:** Review key sections with your client and team before submitting.
- **Final versions due in Sprint 7:** Use the feedback you receive to create polished, client-ready documents.
