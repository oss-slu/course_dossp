## Community Building Checkpoint

**Sprint:** 3
**Focus:** Community Engagement

### Overview

Open source projects succeed when they build active, engaged communities around them. This checkpoint requires you to execute a specific community-building initiative and reflect on what you learned about your project's community needs and strategy.

### Requirements

## Execute a community-building activity, then assess and reflect:

- **Take Action** Do something that builds community around your project:
- Improve contributor documentation or create a "good first issue" guide
- Build or enhance your project website
- Create branding materials or establish social media presence
- Write/publish a blog post or create a demo video
- Implement issue templates or contribution guidelines
- Respond substantively to external issues/questions/PRs
- Conduct user interviews or gather community feedback
- Host or participate in a community discussion or event
**Note:** Activities can be virtual (documentation, websites, branding) or interactive (conversations, events, outreach). The ideal activity genuinely builds community and goes beyond unidirectional marketing campaigns.
- **Reflect and Assess** (1-2 pages) **If you have an existing community strategy:**
- What did you do and why (based on your strategy)?
- Does this advance your user community, contributor community, or both?
- What response or impact did you observe?
- How well did this activity advance your overall community-building objectives?
- What did you learn about what's involved, who's interested, and what factors matter for your community?
- What should you do next?
**If you don't have a community strategy:**
- What did you do and why?
- What did you learn about what's involved, who might be interested, and what factors you need to consider?
- Based on this experience, draft a brief community strategy: Who are you trying to reach? (users, contributors, both?) What unites them? What differentiates them?
- What are 2-3 high-impact next steps?
Your reflection should function as an informal audit and assessment of the community-building work that can or should be done. Doing something gives you real data about your community context—use that to inform your thinking about strategy and next steps.

### Deliverables

- Link to your community-building activity (published post, merged PR, website, documentation, etc.)
- Reflection document (1-2 pages) addressing the prompts above

### Evaluation Criteria

- **Authenticity** Activity and reflection are grounded in your actual project context, not generic advice
- **Evidence & Insight**: Thoughtful execution of the initiative and informed assessment based on what you learned
- **Strategic Thinking**: Clear connection between your activity, what you learned, and your project's community needs

### Notes

- Guest speakers throughout the semester will discuss community—use those insights to inform your approach
- Depth should match your project's maturity
- Your client may offer guidance, but you lead this work
